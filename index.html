Topic 1 • Indefinite Pronouns

SOME- vs. ANY- (People, Things & Places)

Match the ending to the category, and the prefix to the sentence type:

  • ENDINGS: -one / -body = People (someone, anyone) • -thing = Things (something, anything) • -where = Places (somewhere, anywhere).
  • SOME-: Used in Affirmative (+) sentences AND polite Offers/Requests!
    Ex: I heard someone knocking. / Would you like something to drink?
  • ANY-: Used in Negative (-) sentences (with not, couldn't, didn't, doesn't) and general Questions (?).
    Ex: I couldn't see anyone. / He doesn't want to go anywhere expensive. / Did anyone call?
Topic 2 • Modals of Deduction

MUST BE, MIGHT BE, or CAN'T BE?

Look at the evidence in the sentence to decide how sure you are:

  • MUST BE: 99% Sure it is TRUE (strong logical evidence).
    Clues: "long line outside ➔ It must be popular" / "Her name is on the cover ➔ It must be Mary's notebook!"
  • MIGHT BE: 50% Possibility / Unsure (maybe yes, maybe no).
    Clue: Look for "I'm not sure" or "maybe". Ex: She might be taking a shower, but I'm not sure.
  • CAN'T BE: 99% Sure it is IMPOSSIBLE (contradicts the evidence).
    Clue: Wearing a winter coat on a freezing day ➔ He can't be feeling hot!
Topic 3 • Question Tags

Question Tags: The Mirror & Flip Rule!

Keep the same verb tense/auxiliary, but flip the sign: (+) ➔ (-) and (-) ➔ (+).

1. Repeat the Auxiliary / Modal:
• You are afraid..., aren't you? / They aren't from Canada, are they?
• He can't speak..., can he? / They have visited..., haven't they?
2. No Auxiliary? Use DO / DOES (Present) or DID (Past):
• Present: Australians speak..., don't they? / Your sister speaks..., doesn't she?
• Past (-ed): Sarah enjoyed the concert, didn't she?
⚠️ TEST TRAP with "NEVER": Notice how "Peter has never traveled abroad, hasn't he?" or negative auxiliaries work on your review sheet—always match the exact auxiliary verb (has/have, can, do/does, did, are)!
Topic 4 • Reflexives & Vocabulary

Reflexive Mirror vs. Each Other + Vocab

REFLEXIVE PRONOUNS (Self / Selves): Subject & object are the SAME!
• I ➔ myself | You (1) ➔ yourself | He ➔ himself | She ➔ herself
• We (My friends and I) ➔ ourselves | You (2+) ➔ yourselves | They (The students) ➔ themselves.
EACH OTHER (Mutual / Reciprocal Action): A does to B, and B does to A!
Ex: Trust each other / Boys helping each other / Parents help each other.
🎯 Key Vocabulary (Objects & Culture): • Objects: hammer (nails/wood), ball (round toy), paint pot (keep paint), banjo (string instrument), mouse/rat (cheese), banana (smoothies), teapot (hot tea).
• Culture: on a budget (save money), stereotypes (unfair ideas), bow (Japan greeting), kiss on the cheek (grandmother), shake hands (formal meeting).
